# Router comparison, 2026-09-23

Same 36 prompts (`evals/prompts.jsonl`), same three questions and rules (`src/trivium/targets.yaml`
at this commit), confidence thresholds off (`min_confidence: {}`) so every router is judged on its
first choice.

**Machine:** Apple M5 Pro, 48 GB, macOS. **Runtimes:** semif at commit `1f2dea3` with MLX 0.32.2,
Qwen3.5-4B at revision `851bf6e`, bf16. Laya 0.3.7 from PyPI on PyTorch MPS.

With the default thresholds (tuned for semif), semif scores 80.6% right target with 2.8% of prompts
sent to the fallback. Laya English scores 41.7% with 27.8% sent to the fallback: its probabilities are
flatter, so semif's thresholds do not fit it.

## Observations

- Laya answers "moderate" to nearly every difficulty question (probabilities around 0.45-0.65), so the
  easy-to-local and hard-to-frontier rules almost never fire for it.
- Laya's English checkpoint reads chat-style requests as code: "rewrite this sentence to sound less
  formal" became `code_change` at 0.83, "write the changelog entry" at 0.86.
- Laya is the better judge of the yes/no `tools` question (97-100% against semif's 81-83%). A hybrid
  that asks Laya about tools and semif about kind and difficulty is an obvious next experiment.
- Laya loaded with a warning that its English checkpoint ships an out-of-range temperature for
  choice questions with 11+ options (clamped to 0.5). None of these questions has more than 6 options.
- Caveats: 36 prompts, one labeller, and the question wording was tuned on this set with semif. Laya's
  own documentation says it is meant to be fine-tuned before production use; none of this tests a
  fine-tuned Laya.
